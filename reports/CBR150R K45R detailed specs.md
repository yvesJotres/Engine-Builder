# Secondary specs anchor the K45R engine, not the drivetrain

**The K45R engine core is consistent across dealer and press sources: 149.16 cc, 57.3 x 57.8 mm bore and stroke, 11.3:1 compression, DOHC with four valves, liquid cooling, and PGM-FI.** The published peaks are 17.1 PS at 9,000 rpm and 14.4 Nm at 7,000 rpm, but an independent on-wheel dyno measured 15.10 PS and 13.29 Nm, so the claims cannot be used unadjusted for a model. The drivetrain is the weak point: no K45R gear ratios, primary reduction, final-drive ratio, sprocket counts or chain link count were found in any source reached. Chassis figures are usable for wheelbase (1,310 mm), tyres (100/80-17 front, 130/70-17 rear) and tank (12 L), but curb weight (137 to 151 kg), seat height (782 to 788 mm) and height (1,077 to 1,080 mm) remain contested. No Honda (AHM) or Astra Honda primary document was reachable, so every figure below is secondary. Figures from the older K45G, K45N and K45A generations were excluded from K45R values and are listed only where they explain a contradiction.

## Engine figures rest on dealer blocks and one independent dyno

The displacement, bore, stroke and compression set appears on two dealer pages, a press snippet and a dyno article: **57.3 x 57.8 mm, 149.16 cc and 11.3:1** ([Honda Cengkareng CBR 150R](https://www.hondacengkareng.com/motor/honda-cbr-150r/); [Honda Mandala Jaya Abadi](https://www.hondamandalajayaabadi.com/spesifikasi-honda-cbr150r.html); [Otomotifnet dyno article](https://otomotifnet.gridoto.com/read/232535641/all-new-honda-cbr150r-dites-dyno-ternyata-tenaga-on-wheel-segini?page=all)). Dealer spec blocks are not K-coded, so they are strong secondary evidence rather than AHM data. Wikipedia's merged table lists 63.5 x 47.2 mm and 11.0:1 for older columns, and its prose calls the earlier engine short-stroke, which contradicts its own table; those figures are not K45R data ([Wikipedia, Honda CBR150R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_CBR150R)).

The power and torque picture is less settled. The claimed **12.6 kW (17.1 PS) at 9,000 rpm and 14.4 Nm (1.47 kgf.m) at 7,000 rpm** appear on both dealer pages. The Otomotifnet Dynojet 250i test, published 31 January 2021 with no test date, conditions or correction factor stated, measured **15.10 PS at 9,040 rpm and 13.29 Nm at 7,560 rpm** on the rear wheel, with the limiter near 10,600 rpm. The measured torque peak sits about 560 rpm above the claimed peak, and the measured power is about 12% below the claim. The article does not state a correction standard, so the cause of the gap is inferred (drivetrain loss is the usual explanation) and not confirmed. A press snippet (Bikeadvice) gives 17.1 PS at 10,500 rpm and 13 Nm at 7,500 rpm, which conflicts with both the dealer and dyno power peak rpm; it was not fetched. Wikipedia's 18 hp, 14.4 Nm figure has no rpm and is treated as a contradiction. The 12.6 kW and 13.7 Nm pair from the 2016 AHM launch belongs to the K45G generation and must not be used for K45R ([Honda Cengkareng 2016 article](https://www.hondacengkareng.com/ahm-rilis-all-new-honda-cbr-150r/)). No rpm-by-rpm curve exists in any source reached.

Other engine facts are dealer-sourced: full transistorized ignition, NGK MR9C-9N or ND U27EPR-N9 spark plugs, 1.1 L of oil (oil grade not found), and a wet clutch with a 1-N-2-3-4-5-6 shift pattern ([Honda Cengkareng](https://www.hondacengkareng.com/motor/honda-cbr-150r/)). A search snippet from Autofun calls the 2021 model air-cooled, which contradicts the dealer "liquid cooled with auto fan" entry. The Autofun page returned HTTP 403, so the dealer data is adopted and the conflict is flagged rather than resolved. The **throttle body bore for K45R is not found**; the only throttle figure (30 mm venturi, reamed to 33 mm) belongs to the sibling CB150R ([Gridoto](https://www.gridoto.com/read/222473816/honda-pcx-dan-adv150-pasang-throttle-body-reamer-segini-harganya)).

## Drivetrain has no verified ratios and no sprocket data

The K45R is a 6-speed manual, and the 2021 Repsol listing and Wikipedia both confirm this with no ratios attached ([motorcyclespecs.co.za, CBR150R rep 21](https://www.motorcyclespecs.co.za/model/Honda/honda_cbr150r_rep_21.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_CBR150R)). Two numeric gear sets exist in the results, but both belong to older generations: 3.038 / 1.941 / 1.500 / 1.227 / 1.041 / 0.923 on a 2006 to 2007 listing (taken from a search snippet, not fetched), and 1st 3.083, primary 3.260, final 2.933 on a 2010 Bikes Republic page ([Bikes Republic 2010](https://www.bikesrepublic.com/bm/2010-honda-cbr150r-technical-specification)). These two sets already disagree on first gear, so neither can stand in for K45R. The final drive is a chain, with the type and link count unstated, and the Wikipedia entry covers all generations at once. The drivetrain therefore cannot be modelled from verified data; ratios, primary reduction and sprocket counts all require the Honda K45R service manual or a parts catalogue, and no source reached gave gear tooth counts for any generation.

## Chassis masses disagree by up to 14 kg

Curb weight is the most contested chassis figure. Two 2021-specific sources, Autofun (search snippet) and Zigwheels (fetched review), give **137 kg non-ABS and 139 kg ABS** ([Zigwheels](https://www.zigwheels.co.id/motor-baru/honda/cbr150r/)). Motorradundreisen gives 137 kg for 2022 and 138 kg for 2023 ([Motorradundreisen](https://www.motorradundreisen.de/motorraddatenbank/honda/cbr-150r-12361.html)). Wikipedia's 2021 column gives **151 kg**, and a Bangladesh listing claims 157 kg with no market or year. A 135 kg figure appears on an older listing, and an Indian listing gives 138 kg as a snippet. The 151 kg outlier is most likely a different measurement basis or a table-mapping error, but this is an inference. Dry weight, axle split and the weighing standard (fluids and fuel state) were not found anywhere. The wheelbase of **1,310 mm** is consistent across the motorcyclespecs page, Wikipedia and a generic spec page, and tyres of **100/80-17 front and 130/70-17 rear** are consistent across three sources, with the Philippine Wheeltek sheet adding load indices (52P and 62P, tubeless) that are unverified for K45R. Brakes are single 220 mm wave discs; ABS is listed as optional on one 2021 page and standard on a 2023 entry, which is unresolved.

Overall dimensions have less agreement than the wheelbase suggests. Length of 1,983 mm and width of 700 mm agree on the 2021 sources, while 694 mm is the prior generation. Height splits three ways: **1,077 mm** (Wikipedia 2021 column), **1,080 mm** (Autofun snippet and Zigwheels) and **1,038 mm** (an older Cermati listing). Seat height is 782 mm on the motorcyclespecs and Wikipedia data and 788 mm on motorradundreisen, with 787 mm as a K45N/K45G figure. Ground clearance is most likely 160 mm, although 151 and 166 mm both appear. The **fuel tank is 12 L** on the 2021 sources. The drivetrain notes also record 13.1 L and 13 L values from an older Indonesian detik spec and a 2022 to 2024 Indian listing, so the dimensions file's claim that 12 L is "consistent across all sources" overstates the evidence. Frame material and fuel-cap location are not confirmed, and no component masses (crank, piston, flywheel, wheels, chain, exhaust) were published.

The following could not be obtained from any source reached: the K45R service manual or owner's manual (valve clearance, idle, cam timing, ignition advance); K45R gear ratios and primary reduction; sprocket teeth, chain size and link count; intake and exhaust valve diameters and lift; throttle body bore; injector specification; official idle speed; official redline (the 10,600 rpm limiter is a press figure, not Honda's); engine oil grade; dry weight; wheel and tyre masses; and any rear-wheel dyno with correction factors. Honda Indonesia, AHM and Astra Honda pages returned HTTP 403 or were unreachable, and the K45R parts catalogue was not located.

## Next steps

The first priority is an AHM or Honda Indonesia K45R owner's or service manual PDF, which would close the idle, redline, valve, cam, ignition, throttle body and sprocket gaps in one document. Second, the full Otomotifnet article should be retrieved to get the test date and conditions, and a K45R dyno from Motoplus, Oto or Motorplus with correction factors would resolve the power claim. Third, a weighed K45R (dry and wet, per axle) would settle the 137 to 151 kg conflict. Until then, any model should use the 57.3 x 57.8 mm / 149.16 cc / 11.3:1 engine, the dyno peaks as the calibration target with the claims as an upper bound, and treat drivetrain ratios as an open input.
