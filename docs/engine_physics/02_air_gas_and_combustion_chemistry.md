# 2. Air, gas and combustion chemistry

[Index](README.md) · Physics reference

**Air composition (dry, sea level) [S]:** about 20.95 % O₂, 78.08 % N₂, 0.93 % Ar, 0.04 % CO₂ by volume. Two sources agree (product data; Engineering ToolBox gives 20.946 / 78.084 / 0.934 %). CO₂ is quoted between 0.031 % and 0.041 % depending on the year of the source. Molar mass 28.96–28.97 kg/kmol [S].

**Ideal gas relation:** p V = m R T, with R_air = 287.05 J/(kg·K) [D, R_air = R_universal / M_air].

**Specific heat ratio:** γ = c_p / c_v ≈ 1.40 for air near 300 K [T]. Combustion products have lower γ, typically about 1.25–1.35 at combustion temperatures, and it varies with temperature [T].

**Speed of sound:** a = √(γ R T). At 300 K and γ = 1.4, a ≈ 347 m/s [D].

**Air density at standard conditions:** 1.225 kg/m³ at 15 °C, 101.325 kPa (ISA); about 1.18 kg/m³ at 25 °C [T, ideal gas with R_air; 1.184 at 25 °C by calculation, D].

**Stoichiometric combustion of iso-octane (reference fuel):**

C₈H₁₈ + 12.5 (O₂ + 3.76 N₂) → 8 CO₂ + 9 H₂O + 47 N₂

Stoichiometric air–fuel ratio (mass): about 15.1 for iso-octane [D, from the equation above with standard molar masses]; about 14.5–14.7 for typical pump gasoline [T].

**Equivalence ratio and lambda:**
- λ = (A/F)_actual / (A/F)_stoich
- φ = 1 / λ
- Rich: λ < 1. Lean: λ > 1.

**Products beyond stoichiometric:** at rich mixtures CO and H₂ appear; at lean mixtures the products are close to CO₂ and H₂O with excess O₂. At high temperature some dissociation occurs (CO₂ ↔ CO + ½O₂, H₂O ↔ H₂ + ½O₂), which lowers the peak temperature and changes γ [T].

**Knock:** end-gas autoignition ahead of the flame. Rated by octane number. Limits spark advance and compression ratio at high load [T].
